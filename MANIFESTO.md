# The AgentsPoppy Manifesto

**What we're for. And what we refuse.**

This is also on [agentspoppy.com](https://agentspoppy.com/#manifesto). It lives here too because
the people most likely to want sections V and VI — the ones about how this is paid for, and why
there is a patent on an otherwise open project — usually arrive at the repository, not the
website.

---

## I. The cloud belongs to the people who use it.

Software should answer to the person it serves. We build so that apps run in **their users' own
clouds** — legible, pausable, removable — and so that no company database has to exist in the
middle of anyone's private life.

## II. Privacy for people. Not impunity.

Privacy here means **your data answers to you** — it does not mean darkness. We will not let this
platform shelter criminal activity, child exploitation, or illegal trade behind the word
"privacy". Our architecture is the enforcement: **every approved poppy's code is open**, so what
an app *does* is public even when your data is private; the directory is curated, reports are
acted on, and a bad poppy can be blocked and torn out of every cloud it touched — by the very
users it betrayed.

## III. A future for the builders AI displaces.

AI is about to displace millions of developers and white-collar workers from corporations that
will no longer need them. We refuse to treat that as someone else's problem. AgentsPoppy is built
to be **their next serious opportunity**: a place where one person with an idea and an AI agent
can ship — and own — what once required a funded team and millions in infrastructure. We didn't
build this to accelerate the loss of livelihoods. We built it to hand the means of shipping
software to the people automation displaces.

## IV. Claims you can check.

Every promise we make is designed to be verifiable: open source by rule, updates you can diff and
rebuild, a map of everything an app does in your account. **Where we haven't earned a claim yet,
we say so.** Trust that can't be checked is just marketing.

## V. A business that isn't built on your data.

Most free software is free because the user is the product. We wanted to test whether another
shape works: the app is free, your data never reaches our servers, and **there is no database of
yours for us to mine, lose, or sell**. We earn only when someone chooses to buy something. We
can't prove yet that this pays for itself at scale, and we won't pretend otherwise — but we'd
rather find out than reach for the default. The bargain runs both ways. Because the machine and
the cloud are yours, you're owed a plain view of what every app, and every update, puts on them.

## VI. Why there's a patent, and a licence with teeth.

Patents and restrictive licences don't usually belong on a project like this, so here is why we
have them. The valuable thing here isn't our code. It's the rules. **Every app must publish its
source. Every update must be readable before it runs. Removing an app must really delete it.**
Those rules are why any of this can be trusted, and they cost real money and time to keep. Rules
only work if someone has to follow them. If this idea catches on, the tempting move for a bigger
company is to copy the convenient part — apps that install themselves into your own cloud — and
quietly leave the rules behind. That is the version we don't want to become normal. So: read this
code, run it, change it, host it yourself, and build anything you like on top. The one thing you
can't do is take it and sell the same product with the rules removed. That is all the patent and
the licence are for. We might be wrong to bother with them. If these rules spread on their own,
without us, then we got what we wanted.
